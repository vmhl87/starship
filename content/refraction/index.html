An inflection point
Jan 8 2025
10:58 PM

We humans are so good at living in the moment.
Perhaps too good.
- Anonymous


For many reasons, I've recently come to realize exactly how significantly the relationship I had last year - as a whole, not just the breakup - has enabled my ability to understand myself. It's not a coincidence, nor is it bias in recall, that following the breakup in February I began to truly engage in deep discussion with other people for the first time.

A few months ago, I thought that this was simply because the breakup gave me a mess of feelings so important to me, so private, but yet subtly unresolved, that I felt the need to form a distinction between a 'regular' conversation and a 'truly deep' one. But looking back, that wasn't true.


The more I analyze it, the more I realize that the very act of having that relationship was the key that opened up the possibility of a genuine, unfiltered conversation. Those types of genuine interactions crucially depend on interpersonal trust. For me, to open up in such a way meant I needed to trust the people I was with, to know that I could express my true will without having to moderate. This strength of connection, which (to be very revision-5 about this) rougly corresponds to the 'ring 1' of my social web, was a construct that I only began to understand because of my relationship. Prior to that experience I had never held any form of social relationship with another party in which I could truly trust them, and so - as referenced in a few other entries here - dating provided the context to that entirely new sector of my state sapce, the necessary foundation for these sorts of revelations down the line.

Part of this phenomenon originates from the way that I approached social life as a whole. School defined my social sphere for the longest time, and my complicated situation within it henceforth created the baseline of how I see myself socially. I entered the school environment as essentially a fully independent party; I was so different from my peers that I could not ever see myself as truly on the same social level. And so I did not grow to trust them in the way that other 'normal' people grew to trust their friends.

When I crossed the boundary from middle school to high school, from kid to teenager, I tried to branch out more. Entering ninth grade with the goal to get to know more people, to seek out more oppurtunities, I was able to change my degree of connection with my peers, but my fundamental worldview and perception of my friends did not. I still didn't trust them enough to truly act myself. I didn't develop deep connections because my mind was still playing volleyball with the set of social masks I used to 'fit in'. What's more, the barrier went both ways: I didn't get to know anyone well enough to understand them, nor did I put in effort to help my friends understand me.


All of these missing modes of interaction I only learned through my relationship. Looking back, though I didn't necessarily lead with it in the very beginning, that sense of connection and true understanding was precisely what I was seeking. It was the fulfillment I was receiving from the relationship, not necessarily love: the two are closely connected, but while I would have been satisfied with the connection even in the absence of attraction itself, attraction lacking connection was what left me in the final few weeks feeling unfulfilled, suspecting the end.

What's more, in the months following the breakup, I moved through several cycles of feeling attraction to girls, implicitly for the purpose of seeking out such a connection, though I wasn't self-aware at the time.

Crucially, if all I was ever truly seeking in a relationship was a deep personal connection, there is no reason why should be unable to obtain it from a classical friendship. This turns out to have been very much the case, with recent events: I went into December looking for love, and came out with a friendship in which I had formed a genine sense of understanding. And more: I was gifted the understanding of my own self, without which this reflection wouldn't have been possible.

And that is the realization I've made. That my first relationship was much more than just a first relationship, but the beginning of a fundamental shift in the way I live. An inflection point, if you will.
