I've been curious about the world for as long as I can remember. I've been trying to learn as much as I can about the world since I was very young, and I'm drawn to scientific research for two reasons: First of all, discovery is in my opinion one of the most rewarding experiences, and I believe the ultimate form of discovery comes from research, where one can push the boundaries of the entirety of human knowledge. Secondly, I hope that I can somehow pass on this wonderful feeling of curiosity and discovery to other people in the future, and I might be able to achieve this through research.

At PGSS, I hope that I can expand my knowledge, by learning from experts, as well as meet other people similar to myself.

Although I can (and most of the time do) learn on my own, I find learning from teachers or mentors really valuable because I can learn from them not only the course material, but more importantly the way that they understand the material and go about problem-solving. For example, my AP Chemistry teacher is one of my favorite teachers, because he goes out of his way to form as many connections within the content as possible, and explains the thought process and connections he makes while solving problems, instead of just writing out the solution.

Learning connections instead of just facts is very important to me because of how I have (and still do) learn. I started learning science not from school, but by reading various books from the library and that my parents had around the house. I didn't focus on any topics in particular, instead learning about everything, because, well, everything was interesting! Since I wasn't following any sort of structured curriculum, I formed my own connections between everything I was learning, in order to make sense of all of the 'random' facts I was taking in. Over time I built up and kept adding more and more knowledge into this giant 'web' of connections in my head. Unlike the way that science is taught in school, I never developed a notion of 'different' subjects like physics vs. chemistry vs. biology; rather, everything was connected together through some way or another.

So, whenever I'm learning something new, if I can see how the teacher connects it to other concepts in their brain, I pick up and more 'deeply' understand the concept, too. I don't unfortunately find teachers who do this very often at my school, so I'm hoping this will be something I can gain from PGSS.

I'm also hoping I can find a community. Although my school has a fairly high number of students who do STEM, very few are truly interested in learning for learning's sake. Through various competitive programming and competitive math communities online, I've met some people who share this same curiosity, but unfortunately I find that most people who specialize in these subjects tend to not have much interest in the other STEM subjects. I am hoping that PGSS will attract other generalists like me!

I believe I'm a valuable candidate because I share PGSS's core values: to find high school students who love learning, bring them together, and build a community. I'm not looking for an entry to put on my resume, or an opportunity to 'get ahead' of my peers - I truly do love learning, love meeting people like myself, and most of all, want to be part of something bigger than myself.
